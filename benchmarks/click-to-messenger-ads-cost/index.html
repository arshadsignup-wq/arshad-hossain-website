Click-to-Messenger ad costs: $0.02 to $0.04 per conversation.

Observed costs from three click-to-Messenger campaigns I ran in Bangladesh, including one that produced 12,813 conversations for $576.51 — and why these figures don't travel.

Key figures

  • In fashion e-commerce campaigns I ran in Bangladesh during 2025, click-to-Messenger conversations cost between $0.02 and $0.04 each.
  • The largest single campaign produced 12,813 conversations for $576.51 — about four cents per conversation — reaching 514,208 people.
  • A second campaign averaged $0.03 per conversation across 16 creatives and 4 ad sets, on $135.49 of spend.
  • The best-performing single ad set reached $0.02 per conversation.
  • For the same client, a website conversion campaign produced 121 purchases at $0.71 average — a different objective doing a different job, and not directly comparable.

What this is, and what it isn't

  • This is Bangladesh data. These costs come from a conversational-commerce market where a large share of retail already happens inside a Facebook inbox. They are not a global benchmark and should not be quoted as one — North American and European click-to-Messenger costs are materially higher.
  • n = 3 campaigns for one fashion e-commerce brand, run across Eid seasons in 2024–2025. Seasonal demand inflates performance relative to an ordinary month.
  • Figures are USD as reported in Meta Ads Manager. The additional-sales figure on the reel campaign is in Bangladeshi taka, as the client reported it.
  • A conversation is not a sale. Cost per messaging conversation is a top-of-funnel metric. Without the conversation-to-order rate it says nothing about revenue.
  • US lead-generation costs are deliberately kept off this page — see the cost per lead benchmarks instead.

How much do click-to-Messenger ads cost?

In the Bangladeshi campaigns documented above, between $0.02 and $0.04 per messaging conversation. That figure is unusually low because it comes from a market where buying already happens in an inbox — products shown on a Facebook page, terms agreed in Messenger, payment on delivery. Facebook-native retail in Bangladesh has been measured in the thousands of crore taka annually alongside conventional e-commerce [1].

In markets where the norm is a website checkout, expect materially higher costs. The mechanism travels; the pricing does not.

Why conversations are so much cheaper than conversions

Three reasons, in order of how much they matter. First, the action is smaller — opening a chat asks less of someone than completing a checkout, so more people do it. Second, where cash on delivery is the norm, buyers expect to ask questions before committing, so the conversation is a step they wanted anyway rather than friction you introduced. Third, messaging conversions are counted inside Meta's own surface rather than reported back from a browser, so the optimisation signal is short and hard to lose — unlike website conversion tracking, which has been degraded by tracking restrictions for years.

The trap in this metric

Cheap conversations look extraordinary on a dashboard and can still be worthless. Cost per messaging conversation is a proxy: unless you track how many conversations become confirmed orders, a low figure can simply mean a badly qualified audience. On the largest campaign above the client brought in extra staff to handle the inbox — if you cannot staff replies during campaign hours, don't buy the conversations.

What this doesn't mean

It doesn't mean click-to-Messenger will produce four-cent conversations for you. It means that in a market where buying is conversational, matching the campaign objective to that behaviour beat forcing the same budget through a website funnel. The transferable finding is the principle, not the price.

Questions about these figures

How much does a Facebook Messenger conversation cost?

Between $0.02 and $0.04 in the Bangladeshi fashion e-commerce campaigns documented on this page, with the largest producing 12,813 conversations for $576.51. These are conversational-commerce market figures and will not hold in markets where website checkout is the norm.

Are click-to-Messenger ads cheaper than conversion ads?

Per action, almost always — but they buy a different action. In the campaigns here, conversations cost around $0.04 while website purchases for the same brand cost $0.71. A purchase is worth far more than a conversation, so the comparison only makes sense once you know your conversation-to-order rate.

Do these costs apply outside Bangladesh?

No. Treat them as evidence that the objective works where buying is conversational, not as a price you should expect. Anyone quoting four-cent conversations as a global benchmark is misreading this data, and I'd rather say so than let the number travel unqualified.
