When Messenger ads beat conversion ads

Three conditions. If all three hold, a conversational objective will usually outperform sending the same budget to a product page.

  • The inbox is already the storefront. In markets like Bangladesh a large share of retail happens on Facebook Pages — products shown on the page, terms agreed in Messenger, payment on delivery. Pushing those buyers to a website adds a step rather than removing one.
  • Buyers need to ask before they commit. Where cash on delivery is normal, people want to check the fabric, the fit, the delivery date. A checkout page can't answer that. A person can, in ninety seconds.
  • Your conversion tracking is unreliable. Website campaigns depend on event data surviving the trip back from the browser, and that pipeline has been degraded for years. Messaging conversions are counted inside Meta's own surface, so the optimisation signal is short and hard to lose.

When I'd tell you not to

  • You have a working e-commerce site with prepayment and clean checkout
  • Nobody can reply within minutes during campaign hours — thousands of cheap conversations are worthless if the inbox sits unattended
  • Average order value is high enough that buyers want to research rather than chat
  • You need clean revenue attribution rather than conversation volume

For the same client I also ran a conversion campaign that produced 121 purchases at $0.71 average. Both were profitable. They were doing different jobs.

The trap in the metric

Message conversions are cheap and they look extraordinary on a dashboard. They are a proxy. Unless you're tracking how many conversations become confirmed orders, a low cost per message can hide a badly qualified audience. I'll ask for that conversion rate before either of us celebrates.

Common questions

How much do click-to-Messenger ads cost?

In fashion e-commerce campaigns I ran in Bangladesh during 2025, conversations cost between $0.02 and $0.04 each. The largest single campaign produced 12,813 conversations for $576.51 with 514,208 people reached. Costs in North American and European markets are materially higher — these figures are from a market where conversational commerce is the default, and they should not be read as a global benchmark.

Does this work outside Bangladesh?

The mechanism travels; the pricing doesn't. Any market where buyers expect to negotiate or ask questions before purchase is a candidate — but I'd be misleading you if I implied a US campaign will produce four-cent conversations. What transfers is the principle: match the objective to how buying already happens.

Do I need a chat team?

You need someone replying fast. On the biggest campaign I ran, the brand brought in extra staff to keep up with the inbox. If you can't staff replies during campaign hours, don't buy the conversations — you'll pay for contact you can't convert.

Can this be automated?

Partly. Automated greetings and FAQ replies handle the first exchange, but the orders come from a person answering the specific question. Treat automation as triage, not as the salesperson.
