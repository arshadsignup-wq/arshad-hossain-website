What the work actually involves

In the order I do it, because the order is most of the value.

1. Establish what a lead is allowed to cost

Before touching the account I need two numbers from you: what a customer is worth, and roughly what share of enquiries convert. That gives a ceiling. A $40 cost per lead is excellent for a business closing $3,000 contracts and fatal for one selling a $60 treatment, and most accounts are optimised without anyone having done this arithmetic.

2. Fix the measurement

Pixel and Conversions API together, with event deduplication, verified in Events Manager. Since Apple's tracking changes a meaningful share of conversions never reach Meta through the browser alone, which means the delivery algorithm optimises against an incomplete picture. Plenty of accounts have CAPI "installed" in a way that quietly does nothing. This is the least glamorous step and the one that does the most work.

3. Structure for the budget you actually have

Broad targeting with Advantage+ is good advice on a large budget with strong signal. On $50 a day it isn't, because you never accumulate enough conversions per ad set to exit the learning phase. Small budgets need tighter audiences and fewer ad sets, then broadening as the data builds.

4. Test creative against the objection, not the product

The ads that work usually address the hesitation rather than describing the service. In the hair clinic account the winning angle wasn't the treatment. It was the awkwardness of enquiring about it. I brief and iterate creative; I don't produce video or design, but I'll work with your assets or your designer.

Common questions

What's a good cost per lead on Facebook Ads?

It depends entirely on what a customer is worth to you, but for reference: across five accounts I've managed, cost per lead ranged from $3.91 for a local consumer service to $12.23 for niche B2B. Published industry benchmarks for 2026 put the all-industry average closer to $27, with B2B considerably higher. If you're paying more than a customer is worth, the number is wrong regardless of what any benchmark says.

What's the minimum ad spend that makes sense?

Around $1,000 a month for ongoing management. Below that, the account can't generate enough conversion events for Meta's delivery system to learn, and you end up paying a retainer to fight an algorithm that has no data.

Do you handle the creative?

I direct it and test it: angles, hooks, formats, and what to try next based on what the data says. I don't produce video or graphic design. Most clients either have assets already or a designer I brief.

How long until it works?

You'll have signal within days and a meaningful read within four to six weeks. Anything faster is noise; a good day is not a result.

Do you take a percentage of ad spend?

No. Flat retainer. A percentage model rewards me for telling you to spend more, which is not the incentive you want on the other side of the table.

Want to know what your account is leaking?

Send me the ad account and I'll run the same first pass on it (tracking, structure, creative) and tell you what I'd change first.
