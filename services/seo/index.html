The order the work happens in

Month one — foundations and the Business Profile

For anything local, visibility runs substantially through the Google Business Profile rather than the website. Google describes local ranking as relevance, distance and prominence — you can't change how far a searcher is from you, so the work is the other two. Complete profile, correct primary category, consistent name/address/phone everywhere, plus indexability, mobile rendering, speed and schema on the site itself. Measurement configured before changes, so improvement can actually be attributed.

Month two — pages that match how people search

One page per service, not one page listing services. Each covering what it is, who it suits, what happens, and what it roughly costs. Cost is the question people most want answered and the one businesses most often dodge.

Month three — reviews, citations, iteration

By now Search Console shows which queries you appear for but don't win — impressions without clicks, positions in the 8–15 range. Those are the cheapest wins available because the page already nearly ranks.

Why clinics and fintech specifically

Medical and financial content sits in what Google calls "Your Money or Your Life" territory, where its guidance weights experience, expertise, authoritativeness and trust especially heavily. In practice the things a search engineer calls trust signals are the same things a nervous customer looks for: named practitioners with real credentials, a verifiable address, clear scope, and content attributed to someone qualified. Writing more blog posts doesn't substitute for any of that — which is the mistake most clinics make.

Common questions

How long does SEO take?

Local map-pack movement can show within 30 to 90 days. Competitive organic rankings for high-value terms usually take four to six months of consistent work. The Singapore clinic reached Page 1 in three months, but it started from almost nothing — no penalty to recover from, no legacy of bad links. An established site with existing content and entrenched competitors takes longer, and anyone promising three months without looking at your starting point is guessing.

Do you guarantee first-page rankings?

No. Nobody can, and the ones who do are either buying low-competition terms nobody searches or hoping you won't check. What I'll tell you at the audit is whether the terms you want are realistically winnable and roughly what it would take.

Do you write the content?

I plan it, brief it and edit it. For medical and financial topics the content has to carry a qualified name, which means it needs your practitioner's input — that's an E-E-A-T requirement, not a preference, and outsourcing it entirely to a writer is what makes clinic content fail.

Local SEO or national?

Both, but they're different jobs. Local is Business Profile, reviews, citations and proximity. National is content depth, links and topical authority. A clinic with one location needs the first; a fintech selling nationwide needs the second.
